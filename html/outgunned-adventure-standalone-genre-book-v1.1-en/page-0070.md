# Double Difficulty 

When you’re making an especially complex or dangerous roll, the Director can choose to set a double difficulty for it. This means that the roll will have difficulty equal to **2 Basic** , **2 Critical** , or **2 Extreme** (but never 2 Impossible. Jeez!) 

# Rule of the Dice 

You don’t need to roll dice for everything you do, but once the dice are rolled, there is no going back. 

If you try to lift a car and score an Impossible Success, you’ve done it... don’t ask me how! 

As such, the Director must do more than simply setting a difficulty for the roll. They must also and especially determine when and if you need to roll the dice. 

When you face a roll with double difficulty, you still roll the dice only once, but you are looking to score 2 successes to prevail. And in the event of a failure, you’ll have to deal with 2 different consequences. 

The only consolation is that, if you score at least one of the successes you need, you can avoid one of the consequences. 

This rule is more commonly applied to Reaction Rolls to respond to multiple dangers (like several people shooting at you from all directions). 

Some especially stubborn Enemies will also require rolls with double difficulty in order for you to hit them or to avoid their blows. We will talk more about this in **Section 3: DANGERS & ENEMIES** . 

The Director can also choose to set a double difficulty for Action or Reaction Rolls they deem taxing, or as a way to manage complex situations. 

For example, trying to climb a rock wall while a squad of soldiers is shooting at you could require a roll with double difficulty. The same goes for attempting an extremely long jump, or for trying to swindle someone who is already on the defensive about you.
