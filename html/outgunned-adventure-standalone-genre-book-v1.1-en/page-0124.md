As the burning plane crashes against the side of the mountain, you and your Rival go plummeting through the air, gripping onto the same parachute. Who will manage to get it on? 

Your traveling companion grabs onto the wheel and tries to change course, but you are determined to head straight for the waterfall that keeps getting closer. Who will prevail? 

You grab one of the glasses in front of you and down it in one breath, begging your lucky stars that this is not the poisoned one. What will happen? 

All these nail-biting moments filled with conflict and uncertainty can be resolved with an **Opposed Roll** . 

In an Opposed Roll, two Players simultaneously pick up the dice to determine which of them will prevail. 

This is also the only circumstance when a Director can roll the dice like Adventurers usually do. There’s no end to the surprises! 

# Types of Opposed Rolls 

There are 3 types of Opposed Rolls: 

**Adventurer VS Director:** When the Adventurer is facing off directly against an opponent, or standing against a force of nature, the Director can call for an Opposed Roll to determine the outcome of their efforts. 

This type of challenge is definitely the most common Opposed Roll, and it’s designed to give more weight to a pivotal moment in the story. As a Director, you should opt for this type of roll when you want to extend a moment, giving the impression of things happening in slow-motion. 

When making an Opposed Roll, the Director can roll any number of dice they choose, usually 5, 6, or sometimes even 7, depending on the opponent’s strength and the circumstances. If you feel that 5 dice are too many, perhaps the moment is not dramatic enough to require an Opposed Roll. 

**Director:** _June and the evil Colonel both dash toward the Treasure, but it’s impossible to know who will get there first. It’s the perfect context for an Opposed Roll._
