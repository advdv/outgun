There are things that money can’t buy, like a Treasure, or the dumbstruck expression on your Rival’s face when they realize you've played them once again. For everything else, there's **Cash ($)** ! 

# What Cash is 

Cash represents the money that you carry around, or that you have easy access to. Each $ doesn’t represent a specific sum in dollars, it’s a generic unit that represents your buying power. Banknotes in your wallet, watches you can pawn, and loose change can all be represented by your Cash. 

You can have up to 5$, and you can spend it to buy gear, information, or other useful services. 

# What Cash is NOT 

Cash is not an indicator of your general wealth or social status, but simply a resource you can spend to pay for goods and services while on your adventure. 

Anyway, your Cash doesn’t tell you if you’re rich or not. It simply tells you which benefits you can get by spending the money or pawning the valuables you carry around. 

If you have 1$, you’re not necessarily down on your luck. Maybe you really are poor as a church-mouse, or maybe you’re a wealthy heir who suddenly finds themselves short on cash and who can’t wait to get back to their huge mansion. I wonder how the butler is doing. 

# Earning Cash 

All Adventurers start the game with 1$ in their pocket, and they can decide to spend it immediately to buy some additional gear. Some Roles and Tropes grant you the Moneybags Feat, which grants you additional Cash that you either earned or inherited. 

During your adventure, you can get additional Cash by selling some of your possessions, or simply by borrowing it from your beloved enemies. 

Anyway, it’s hard for an Adventurer to get their hands on more than 1$ at a time, unless they get their hands on a safe or some such thing. And when you re-sell an item, most buyers will only be willing to pay one third of the initial price. For example, it takes 1$ to buy a revolver, but to earn that same 1$ you’ll need to sell 3 revolvers at once. And the buyer will insist they are doing you a favor!
