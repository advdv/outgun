Piling up Problems<br><!-- End of picture text -->

You’re piloting a plane, one engine is failing **AND** you ended up in the middle of a blizzard. You’re plummeting down a waterfall **AND** getting shot at. You are hanging from the edge of an erupting volcano **AND** trying not to lose the map. You know, when it rains, it pours, and when you go off to adventure, problems often pile up on top of each other, they never play fair. 

When the situation gets especially dramatic or complex, the Director can determine that the Adventurer must make an Action or Reaction Roll with **Multiple Difficulty** . 

In such cases, the Director just needs to tack on + Basic, + Critical, or + Extreme to the roll difficulty. For example, a Critical + Basic Action Roll, or a Basic + Extreme Reaction Roll, and so on. 

Let’s say translating a diary requires a Basic Action Roll, translating that diary while someone is trying to snatch it from your hands requires a Basic + Critical Action Roll. Jumping out of a plane during takeoff requires an Extreme Action Roll, but jumping out while dragging an enemy along requires an Extreme + Basic Action Roll. 

The neat thing is that, even if the roll has more than one difficulty, you will still have to roll dice only once and try to meet **both** difficulties in one throw! 

**Alaska:** _When the seaplane is about to take off, I realize there is no time for this. I take a deep breath and dive off the cliff, trying to land on one of the wings._ 

**Director:** _Right as you are about to jump, one of the soldiers notices you and shoots a couple hasty bullets in your direction. Your roll will have multiple difficulty Critical+Basic. You need a Critical Success for the jump and a Basic to dodge the bullets!_ 

**Alaska:** _No sweat! I roll Brawn+Stunt and hope that  Luck still has my back!_
