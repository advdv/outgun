**Adventurer VS Adventurer:** When two Adventurers are in open disagreement, or try to impose their will on one another, the Director may choose to let them make an Opposed Roll. 

An Opposed Roll between Adventurers should only be used to make a plot milestone even more epic, not to solve squabbles between Adventurers (or Players!). If used well, this type of challenge can make for truly emotional scenes, but if you’re making Opposed Rolls between Adventurers every single Session, it might be best if you take one step back. 

**Director:** _Jason is blinded by rage and taking aim for a kill-shot when Alaska grabs onto his arm, trying to make him see reason. This is a good Opposed Roll!_ 

**Adventurer VS Fortune:** When an Adventurer tries something unexpected or flat-out crazy, something not even the Director can set a difficulty or plan an outcome for, you can leave the resolution up to Fortune herself. 

For example, when you jump in front of a train hoping that it will grind to a halt an inch from your face, or if you get the brilliant idea to pull a random lever and hope that it will be the right one, the Director can ask you to make an Opposed Roll against Fortune. 

When facing this kind of challenge, you should first of all choose who, among the other Players, will play the role of Fortune and roll against you. You’re free to act as you wish, choosing the Attributes and Skill you feel are best for the roll, and regardless of your choice, Fortune will roll the same number of dice and enjoy the same advantages that you get, including any Free Re-roll you might have. In short, the playing field between you and Fortune is always level. **Director:** _“Stop making noise!” Raymond shouts, without lifting his eyes from the book. However, he hasn’t realized that the noise coming from behind him is really a huge bear, and it… Honestly, I don't know how it could react! How about we make this an Opposed Roll against Fortune?_
