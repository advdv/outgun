# Gaining an Experience 

When you live through a scene that leaves a mark, you or the Director can choose to turn it into an Experience to note on your Adventurer Sheet. 

For example, when you fall madly in love, when you come out on top after a dramatic situation, when someone sacrifices themselves for you, or when you narrowly escape mortal danger, you clearly deserve an Experience. 

Usually, Experiences are noted down in the moment, during the same Shot when they are lived through. However, in the heat of the moment, it’s understandable if you forget to mark down the Experience, or perhaps even disregard its importance. 

If you haven’t gained any Experiences by the end of a shot, ask yourself: 

- ♦ Did I face my fears or overcome my limits? 

- ♦ Did I get close with someone who is not like me? 

- ♦ Did I learn something that changed me? 

- ♦ Was I ever at risk of not making it? 

- ♦ Did I deal with an event from my past? 

- ♦ Did I spark anger or gratitude in someone else? 

If the answer to at least one of these questions was yes, you should talk with the Director and evaluate if the Experience is worth a place on your Sheet. In any case, you don’t need to gain an Experience every Shot, and there is nothing wrong if you have nothing to note down at the end of a session. 

An Adventurer should earn at least one Experience during a **Cinematic Campaign** , but they can also get more than one. 

If, when you pass a **Point of No Return** , there are some Adventurers who still haven’t gained an Experience, this might mean that they still haven’t had a chance to shine and prove their value. It’s the duty of a good Director to keep track of who has earned an Experience and who hasn’t, so as to give every Adventurer the right space and time on the scene. 

Finally, any time an Adventurer who was **Left for Dead** comes back into the action, they always suffer a Scar connected to their demise.
