# No-Ride Chase 

Most chases involve people driving a car or motorcycle, or in corner cases a speedboat, a plane, or a helicopter. 

However, there are chases that involve no ride at all. For example, when you and your friends are chasing after your enemies on foot, moving over the rooftops of a city, or when you’re swimming at top speed to avoid getting eaten by a shark. And how could we forget the high-speed chases made skiing down a mountain? 

Not all chases need to be managed with the rules you have just read. Indeed, you can often simply use some Action and Reaction Rolls to set the scene for fast-paced, adrenaline-filled chases. 

However, if you want to give more weight to a chase on foot, you can assign a Need score to it and manage it just like you would a car chase. 

In such cases, the Heroes’ Speed represents their speed as a group, and all participants need to make an Action Roll on their turn, unless they choose to stop. 

When a Hero has lost all their Grit, it means they were left behind. 

During a no-ride chase, the Heroes’ starting speed is always 0 unless the Director decides to grant them a +1 for special circumstances. 

# carmageddon 

Some chases are truly brutal, and the only way to get home in one piece is to eliminate all opponents. 

When there’s no way to outrun your pursuers and the only solution is resorting to violence, the Director can choose to ignore Need and Speed and manage the scene as combat. 

In this case, the rides chasing after you are full-blown Enemies. 

It goes without saying, but in a no-ride chase there is no driver.
